# Day 07: The Immune System

Date: 25/10/2026

## Big idea
The immune system is the body's defence network. It detects anything that
doesn't belong (bacteria, viruses, toxins, abnormal cells) and destroys it,
while leaving the body's own cells alone.

## Key terms
- **Pathogen**: a disease-causing microbe (bacterium, virus, fungus, parasite)
- **Antigen**: a molecule (usually a protein or sugar on a pathogen) that the immune system recognises
- **Antibody**: a Y-shaped protein that binds one specific antigen
- **Immunity**: the body's ability to resist an infection

## Two lines of defence

### 1. Innate immunity (fast, general, no memory)
- Barriers: skin, mucus, stomach acid, tears
- White blood cells that eat invaders: **neutrophils**, **macrophages**
- **Inflammation**: redness, heat, swelling bring more immune cells to the site
- **Fever**: slows the growth of many microbes
- **Interferons**: proteins released by virus-infected cells to warn neighbours

### 2. Adaptive immunity (slower, specific, has memory)
- **B cells** make antibodies
  - Antibodies bind the antigen, block it, and tag it for destruction
- **T cells**
  - **Helper T cells**: coordinate the response
  - **Killer (cytotoxic) T cells**: destroy infected cells
- **Memory cells**: stay after the infection, so a second exposure is
  much faster and stronger

## How a response works (steps)
1. Pathogen gets past the barriers
2. Macrophages engulf it and display pieces of its antigens
3. Helper T cells recognise those antigens and activate B and killer T cells
4. B cells produce antibodies; killer T cells destroy infected cells
5. Infection is cleared; memory cells remain

## Vaccines
- Train the adaptive system using a harmless form of the antigen
  (weakened/killed pathogen, a protein piece, or mRNA that makes the antigen)
- Result: memory cells form without getting the disease

## Antibodies are proteins (link to Day 5)
- Their shape (structure) decides which antigen they fit
- Same idea as an enzyme and its substrate: shape = function

## Link to Day 6 (bacteria and viruses)
- Bacteria: antibodies and phagocytes attack them; antibiotics also kill them
- Viruses: hide inside our cells, so killer T cells and interferons matter
  more; antibiotics don't work on them

## When the immune system goes wrong
- **Autoimmune disease**: attacks the body's own cells (e.g. type 1 diabetes)
- **Allergy**: overreaction to a harmless antigen
- **Immunodeficiency**: weak response (e.g. HIV destroys helper T cells)
- **Cancer**: abnormal cells that escape detection

## Why it matters for drug discovery
- Monoclonal antibodies are designed drugs that target one specific protein
- Vaccines and immunotherapies are built by understanding antigens
- Antibody and antigen sequences/structures are studied computationally,
  which is where bioinformatics comes in

## Summary (3 lines)
Innate immunity reacts fast and generally; adaptive immunity reacts
specifically and remembers. Antibodies are proteins whose shape matches an
antigen. Vaccines use this memory to prevent disease.
